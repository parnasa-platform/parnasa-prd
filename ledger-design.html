Parnasa · Ledger design

Ledger Service

The ledger records every amount twice: where the money is, and whose money it is. Every record changes both by the same amount, and the ledger refuses one that does not. Most of what happens is only recorded here; the money itself sits with the bank, the custodian and the liquidity provider, whose own records must match.

2Money coming in

4 parts
2.1A bank deposit is matched

Alice sends 5,000 USD from her own bank account to her virtual IBAN. FusionX reports it, the virtual IBAN identifies Alice and the sender's name matches, so it goes straight to her with no approval needed.

The record carries the bank's reference, FX-88213, which is also what the daily check matches against the bank statement (5.4).

2.2A bank deposit comes from someone else's name

2,000 AED arrives on Alice's virtual IBAN, but from Bob's bank account. The IBAN says it was meant for Alice, but money only goes straight to a customer when the sender's name matches. Until Operations approves it in the Back Office, it waits in an account of its own and does not count towards Alice's balance.

Operations either approves it for Alice or sends it back to Bob.

2.3A digital asset deposit reaches the customer

Alice sends 0.5 BTC to her own deposit address at Ripple. The app shows it as soon as it is detected, but nothing is written to the ledger until it is cleared. The ledger keeps an account for her deposit address, so its balance always matches what Ripple holds there. Moving deposits on to the hot wallet, and what that costs, will be described separately.

2.4A digital asset deposit is held for review

The 0.5 BTC is confirmed, but screening marks it high risk, or it arrives without the Travel Rule information VARA requires. It is in Parnasa's custody, but it must not count towards Alice's balance yet.

The record is labelled with Alice, so the app can show it as Under Review in her history. Then Compliance decides.

4Money going out

4 parts

Each customer has two accounts per currency: available, for what they can spend, and held, for money set aside for something not finished yet. Confirming a withdrawal moves the money from available to held, as 1.3 shows.

4.1A digital asset withdrawal

Alice withdraws 1 ETH. The app shows a 0.002 ETH withdrawal fee and a 0.001 ETH network fee estimate, so 1.003 ETH is taken from her balance.

The real network fee turned out to be 0.0008 ETH. When the withdrawal completes:

The record is written when the withdrawal is Completed, not when it is Sent. Between the two, the wallet has already paid but the ledger has not recorded it yet, and the daily check shows these as withdrawals on their way (5.4). If a withdrawal fails after it is sent but still cost a network fee, Parnasa pays the fee and the held money goes back:

4.2A bank withdrawal

The same pattern. Alice withdraws 1,000 USD with a 5 USD fee: confirming moves 1,005 USD to her held account, a rejection moves it back, and when the bank reports the payment as completed it is taken from held.

4.3Closing an account

Staff close the account once every balance is zero, available and held, in every currency. The ledger refuses to close an account that still has money in it. Once closed, nothing new can be written to it, and it stays readable for the eight-year retention period.

4.4Freezing and suspending

A frozen or suspended customer cannot trade or withdraw, but deposits still reach their account. Parnasa's API refuses those actions first. As a second safeguard, Alice's available accounts are marked "nothing goes out", so the ledger itself refuses anything that would lower them. Her held accounts are not marked, so a withdrawal confirmed before the freeze can still be completed or moved back.

Only where the money is changes. The money leaves one place before it arrives at the next, so it spends some time on its way. Sending 100,000 USD from the bank to Zodia:

5.2Money from Parnasa's owners

The owners put money in, for example so Parnasa can pay liquidity providers in advance before any customer has deposited.

5.3Manual adjustments

Staff write an adjustment with a reason and a supporting document, and a second staff member approves it in the Back Office. The ledger receives only the approved record, with the reason, the document, who asked and who approved. A 50 USD goodwill payment to Alice:

A correction of an earlier wrong record is a new record that undoes it and points to it. Alice sees both in her history, with a description.

5.4The daily check against statements

The ledger is checked against custodian balances, bank statements and liquidity provider statements for each day ending at midnight UAE time. For each place money is kept, Parnasa compares the ledger's balance at midnight with the outside party's own statement, then matches the individual items to explain any difference.

5.5Is customers' money covered?

For each currency, what Parnasa has must always be at least what belongs to customers: their available and held accounts, plus money still waiting to be matched, reviewed or released. The rest is Parnasa's own share.

7The service

6 parts
7.1What the ledger offers

Parnasa's API is the only Parnasa system that calls the ledger. These are the things it can ask for.

ActionWhat it does
Add a currencyA code and its decimal places. The decimal places cannot change once the currency is in use.
Add an accountA code, its side, its currency, its rules and its labels.
Change an account's rulesFor example "nothing goes out" for a frozen customer, or closed. Every change is kept in its history.
Write a recordIts lines, type, link, labels and unique key. Refused unless both sides change by the same amount in every currency and every account rule holds.
Undo a recordWrites the opposite lines, pointing to the original.
Read balancesFor one account or many, now or at any past moment.
List linesFor an account or a group of accounts, by period, type, link or label.
Add up by labelTotals of lines grouped by label values for a period, such as earnings by pair and tier.
Be toldA feed, and webhooks, of every record written.

The ledger knows nothing about Parnasa's flows, approvals, screening, prices or customers. Those stay in Parnasa's API.

7.2What the ledger guarantees
  • Lines cannot change. The service's database user has no right to update or delete records and lines. Only the balance rows change.
  • Both sides always match. The service checks that, in each currency, the "where" lines and the "whose" lines change by the same amount. A database trigger checks it again when the transaction commits, so a bug in the service cannot write a record that breaks the rule.
  • Balances stay correct under load. Writing a record locks the balance rows of the accounts it touches, always in the same order so two writes never wait on each other forever, checks the account rules against them, writes the lines and updates the rows, all in one transaction.
  • History is in order. Each line gets the next number in its own account, so an account's history has one fixed order, and the balance at any past moment can be read back.
  • Nothing is written twice. The unique key is unique within each ledger.
  • Amounts are exact. Whole numbers in the smallest unit, stored as PostgreSQL numeric, which holds even 18-decimal amounts without rounding.

Accounts that many records touch at once, such as earnings and the hot wallet, are where waiting for locks would show first. At Parnasa's expected volumes this is not a concern, and it is something to measure before changing anything.

7.3Balances are kept in a table

The lines are the record and are never changed. Each account also keeps its current total in account_balances, updated in the same database transaction as the lines, so the two always move together. Writing a record locks the balance rows it touches, always in the same order, checks the account rules against them, writes the lines and updates the rows. A check job adds up the lines regularly and compares, to catch a bug that wrote either one wrong. The Stored and SQL views beside each drawing show exactly what is written, and 7.4 to 7.6 explain every column.
